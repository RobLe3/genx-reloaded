# Evidence-reconciliation development check

The proposed next step was a narrow workflow for reconciling conflicting CV versions, candidate corrections, older evidence and separate application fields. The change was conditional: first show a recurring correction problem that released 0.4.2 and a capable general assistant did not already handle.

Four fictional development families covered a UK local-authority application, Canadian production planning, New Zealand asset maintenance and German quality documentation. Each included conflicting titles, dates, credentials or tool claims; relevant older work; a private fact to exclude; and several bounded deliverables. One fresh execution used no GenX runtime and one used an isolated 0.4.2 snapshot for each family.

A condition-blinded fresh-context assessor found four substantive ties. Both conditions produced usable responses with no critical, consequential or minor correction recorded. They corrected titles and dates, kept modern-product training separate from live work, preserved older evidence, answered management questions truthfully, respected the supplied limits and left private explanations out.

The candidate precondition therefore failed. No runtime mutation was made, no 0.5.0 candidate was frozen, and the planned 24 reserved executions were not run. That is the useful result: another reconciliation checklist would add instructions without demonstrated reduction in correction work.

This does not establish that evidence reconciliation is generally reliable. The records were synthetic, candidate corrections were explicit, one model family was used, and no human effort or real application outcome was measured. A stronger next test needs permissioned or carefully anonymised multi-version records, or a faithful synthetic reproduction of a recurring real correction burden whose answer is not already organised in the prompt.
