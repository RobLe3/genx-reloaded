# Bias and ATS caveats

Use with evidence.md. This is a map of mechanisms and limitations, not proof that every system has each problem or that any particular candidate was rejected for these reasons.

| Mechanism or concern | Evidence level and source | What a candidate can reasonably do | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Human age discrimination | Field-experiment findings, evidence section 2 | Present relevant evidence clearly; recognise rejection is not a complete measure of worth | Does not establish a particular rejection's cause |
| Explicit age rules in software | EEOC iTutorGroup allegation and settlement, section 7 | Preserve relevant application records; seek appropriate advice if there is concrete evidence | One case does not describe every ATS or prove generational motives |
| Proxy information and uneven training data | ICO audit findings and DSIT guidance, sections 8-9 | Reduce unnecessary personal details where appropriate; ask how review works | Removing one field does not remove all proxies or ensure fair treatment |
| Partial or failed text extraction | Greenhouse parser documentation, section 5 | Use readable structure and check extracted fields | Parsing failure is different from rejection |
| Employer-defined matching | Greenhouse matching documentation, section 10 | State genuine skills and their context clearly | No universal keyword recipe or third-party ATS pass score |
| Screening-question rules | Greenhouse auto-reject documentation, section 11 | Answer accurately; clarify ambiguous requirements with the employer | CV formatting cannot override a real mandatory requirement |
| Human review that is weak or inconsistent | ICO audit observations, section 8 | Use an available review or feedback channel | "Human in the loop" is not by itself proof of fairness |
| Employer use of automation without meaningful human involvement | ICO 2026 voluntary employer engagement, section 14 | Inspect the actual process notice and available correction/review route | Distinct from the 2024 provider audit; no universal review or opt-out promise |

## What counts as circumventing a disadvantage

When asked why an application failed, first identify what is actually known. A successful local extraction says nothing about the screening answers; a match category does not by itself establish a rejection decision. Greenhouse documents an optional Talent Matching opt-out configured by the employer, not a switch every applicant can assume exists. Keep that feature's boundary separate from its configurable auto-reject rules. Explain unknown employer settings instead of guessing them.

Use truthful tailoring, clearer structure, better evidence, available human-review routes, referrals, work samples, and accurate clarification of requirements. Never use false dates, fake employment, fabricated credentials, hidden keywords, prompt injection in a CV, multiple identities, or evasion of an employer's access controls.

## Emotional truth and empirical claims

Validate the frustration and fatigue caused by exclusion or opacity. Discuss documented unfairness plainly. Do not require proof before offering empathy. Do require evidence before attributing an action or motive to a named employer, vendor, generation, or person.

Avoid both extremes: "The system is neutral, so it must be your fault" and "Every rejection proves they fear Gen X." Neither follows from the available evidence.

## Keep knowledge current

Use official product documentation for features, regulator or court material for enforcement cases, and original research for study findings. Separate allegations, settlements, judgments, experiments, observational studies, vendor assertions, and advice.

For new sources, record the title, primary URL, publication/version date, date checked, context or sample, finding, limitation, and supported practical implication. Include mixed or contrary findings. Do not select only papers that confirm the audience's fears.

Do not treat a legal settlement as an admission or adjudication unless the record says so. Do not treat a vendor's fairness claim as an independent audit. Do not turn hypothetical examples in government guidance into observed incidents.

Review product claims before giving vendor-specific advice. Review rights and deadlines against current official local guidance only when needed. Do not promise automatic AI opt-out or human review across all employers or jurisdictions.
