# Working patterns

These are editing templates and fictional examples, not research findings.

## Recover experience without inventing modern credentials

| Original fact | Supported capability | Modern relevance | Unsafe upgrade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scheduled batch workloads and documented recovery steps on a mainframe | Workload operations and recovery procedures | Reliability-focused roles, subject to the target's requirements | Claiming Kubernetes or cloud SRE experience |
| Managed a warehouse relocation with a stated inventory accuracy result | Operational change and inventory control | Operations or logistics delivery | Claiming global supply-chain transformation without that scope |
| Wrote approved procedures for a regulated production line | Controlled documentation and process knowledge | Quality or compliance work in a relevant setting | Claiming a current auditor qualification |
| Managed an on-premises network replacement across named sites | Infrastructure change and migration planning | Architecture and modernisation roles | Relabelling the work as a cloud migration |
| Mentored apprentices and assessed practical tasks | Coaching and skill assessment | Technical leadership and team development | Inventing a people-management position |

Separate direct fit, transferable evidence, and missing experience. Explain the connection rather than laundering a past technology into a current skill.

## Requirement map

| Requirement | Priority | Source evidence and period | Fit | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Multi-site infrastructure delivery | Mandatory | Supplied CV, 11-site network replacement, 2014 | Direct but historical | Retain with original date |
| Kubernetes production operation | Mandatory | No supplied evidence | Missing | Identify gap; do not add to skills |
| Team development | Preferred | Supplied apprenticeship mentoring, 2018-2020 | Direct | Add concise example if target-relevant |

Do not calculate a fit percentage from row counts. Requirements have different significance, and private employer criteria are unknown.

## Evidence and recency record

For consequential edits, use:

**Claim | source passage/file | period | documented/user-stated/needs confirmation | allowed wording.**

Keep ordinary self-reported employment usable. Seek clarification for a conflict or a proposed substantive upgrade, not for every sentence.

Do not combine figures that measure different things, such as test cases, customers, documents, and deployments. Do not assume the largest number is the most useful number.

## Bullet patterns

- **Outcome available:** "[Action] [system/process] across [supported scope], achieving [measured result within its actual conditions]."
- **Outcome unavailable:** "[Action] [concrete deliverable] for [supported context], with responsibility for [supported contribution]."
- **Historical achievement:** Include its role or period; do not imply current tool fluency.
- **Independent work:** Name it as independent work and distinguish prototype, evaluation, release, deployment, and adoption.
- **Collaborative work:** Attribute the person's contribution within the team result.

Fictional before: "Responsible for all IT and digital transformation."

Supported notes: Led six engineers; replaced network at 11 sites; no cost-saving evidence.

After: "Led six engineers through a network replacement across 11 sites."

Do not add "cut costs by 30%" or label the project AI transformation.

Fictional before: "Very experienced quality expert."

Supported notes: Authored 18 approved work instructions, trained 24 operators, certification expired.

After: "Authored 18 approved work instructions and trained 24 operators."

Keep credential validity separate and accurate. Numbers here are fictional and must never be copied into a real applicant's CV.

## Questions that reveal useful evidence

Ask at most three at a time, selected for the target:

- Which decision did you personally make, and what alternatives did you weigh?
- What did you leave behind: a working service, process, design, team, control, or reusable tool?
- What can you substantiate about scope: users, locations, budget responsibility, throughput, or team?
- What changed after your contribution, and how was that observed?
- Which relevant task have you performed recently, and at what depth?
- Which type of responsibility do you want in the next role?

Draft with known facts while awaiting answers. Do not force a financial metric where a concrete operational result is more appropriate.

## First-page test

Read only the opening or first rendered page. Record the exact text supporting:

1. Target role.
2. Current relevant capability.
3. Two or three credible evidence points.
4. Scope or responsibility.
5. Fit with the stated career direction.

Mark "not apparent" rather than importing information from the master history. This is an editorial exercise, not eye-tracking research or a prediction of recruiter behaviour.

## Compact review notes

Prefer this shape after the finished CV:

**Positioning:** one sentence.

| Change | Why it helps | Factual boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Specific before -> after] | [Clarity or relevance] | [Preserved scope/status] |

**Retained historical advantage:** the older evidence worth keeping.

**Open issues:** only facts or requirements that change the application.

**Checks:** source comparison, first-page review, rendered layout, local text extraction, or explicitly not performed.
