# New Zealand

Checked 12 October 2026. Recognise NZ. Use the local application requirements rather than importing Australian or US rules.

## CV and referees

[Tahatū's CV guidance](https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv), updated 29 September 2026, recommends simple presentation, relevant work/volunteer evidence and aiming for two pages where possible. It says to supply referees when the advertisement requests them, otherwise an available-on-request line is acceptable. Treat this as guidance, not a mandatory length or guaranteed ATS recipe.

Use a work-focused CV when it best explains an experienced career. A strengths-led opening may help a returner, but retain truthful employment periods. List original qualifications and actual training, with a plain explanation if unfamiliar. Do not invent an NZQA assessment, New Zealand licence or professional registration. Check the relevant body only when the role needs recognition; assessment, permission to practise and work authorisation are separate questions.

Use New Zealand spelling and preserve names and macrons. Include community or whānau-related experience only when the person chooses and it supports the role. Never invent cultural affiliation, te reo Māori proficiency, a pepeha or Treaty knowledge to match an advertisement. Likewise do not demand private explanations for a career break, even when generic advice suggests discussing them.

[Privacy Commission recruitment guidance](https://www.privacy.org.nz/resources-and-learning/a-z-topics/recruitment/) says employers should contact nominated referees and obtain express consent before approaching others. Ask a referee's agreement before sharing details. If a current boss does not know about the search, offer candidate-approved alternatives or draft a request to agree timing before contact. Do not promise that listing someone leaves contact automatically deferred.

For public-service, regulated or safety-sensitive work, inspect separate statements, checks and eligibility requirements. Recheck current official sources for a concrete rights or recognition question. Do not treat general ATS guidance on a careers page as proof of a particular vendor's ranking or rejection mechanism.
