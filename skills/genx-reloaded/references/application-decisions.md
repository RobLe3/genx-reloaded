# Practical application decisions

Use these editorial patterns when the situation arises. They are not legal rules, ATS guarantees, or additional research findings.

## Language and market

- Follow the requested application language; do not translate an English CV simply because the employer is in Germany.
- If the CV and cover letter use different languages by choice, keep facts aligned and explain the choice briefly only when useful.
- If the user asks for an explanation in a cover letter, put it in the letter itself. Commentary outside the deliverable does not satisfy that request. Use only the stated reason; a neutral "Meinen Lebenslauf reiche ich auf Englisch ein; dieses Anschreiben ist auf Deutsch verfasst" needs no invented international strategy.
- Preserve the original qualification or licensed title. Add a plain-language explanation rather than inventing degree equivalence. Example: "Fachinformatiker Systemintegration, German vocational qualification in IT systems integration" does not mean a bachelor's degree.
- Preserve the language level the person reports. Never infer native fluency from a name, nationality, or birthplace, or upgrade B2 to C1 because a vacancy asks for it.
- Use original job titles with a faithful translation or functional explanation when helpful. Keep target-role headlines separate from previous titles.
- Use readable headings and locally understandable dates. If only years are known, keep years in the CV. Ask before supplying a month required by a form.
- Check current official local guidance before claiming a disclosure, photograph, signature, credential recognition, or format is legally required.

## Career breaks and private explanations

The candidate controls what appears in application materials. A private explanation in conversation is context, not permission to publish it.

Offer "Career break, 2020-2022" if accurate. Use a more specific description such as caring responsibilities only when the candidate wants it. Do not convert unemployment or illness into consultancy. Do not claim training, volunteering, or projects during the break unless supplied.

Keep the candidate's chosen neutral wording consistent across the CV and requested cover note. If a form requests further detail, show the question and help the person choose a truthful response. Do not infer that it is mandatory or lawful merely because it is visible on a website.

## Moving from leadership to hands-on work

Do not assume a former manager wants another management role or is unwilling to take direction. Ask about their desired responsibilities when unclear.

Keep the historical title and actual team or budget scope. Foreground recent individual contribution, supported technical depth, and the candidate's stated reason for the move. Example, only when confirmed: "Seeking a hands-on architecture role focused on design and delivery."

Avoid apologising for age, promising to work cheaply, deleting real responsibility, or describing the person as overqualified as if that were an established fact. A title change alone does not show current tool fluency.

## Evidence without neat metrics

A useful achievement need not include money or a percentage. Approved work instructions, a completed migration, a resolved recurring fault, trained apprentices, a maintained service, and a delivered design can be meaningful when supported.

Keep team results separate from personal ownership. Use "Contributed the test plan to a team migration" when that is the person's role. Preserve conditions for numbers: evaluated on 12 configurations is not 12 production customers; a 30% reduction in one test is not an organisation-wide saving.

Do not request confidential client names, internal financials, security details, or documents the person cannot share. Ask for an approved description or a supported, non-sensitive scope instead. Do not manufacture a rounded metric as a privacy workaround.

## Screening questions and genuine gaps

Check the exact question, including mandatory versus preferred and whether alternatives are stated. "Degree or equivalent experience" is different from "current registration required." Explain the difference without inventing equivalence or legal eligibility.

Answer factual questions accurately. Where a form cannot represent a vocational qualification, expired credential, partial language proficiency, or approximate date, use an available truthful option or draft a clarification to the employer. Never choose a false answer to get past a filter.

For a genuine gap, offer practical choices: apply transparently when reasonable, ask whether equivalence is accepted, pursue a relevant qualification if the candidate wants it, or target a closer role. Do not present unpaid work or expensive retraining as an automatic obligation.

Keep review notes candid about all material gaps. Keep cover letters focused on supported fit and the candidate's intended direction; do not turn them into a catalogue of every preferred skill the person lacks. Always answer explicit factual questions truthfully.

## Finished output and a manageable next step

Keep unresolved claims outside submission copy. A CV without the disputed statistic is often usable while that detail is checked. A missing essential identity, chronology, or qualification fact may leave the document a draft; say exactly what remains.

Give a short readiness note that distinguishes content review, visual inspection, and extraction. Do not let the note become a second CV or a list of every possible hiring risk.

For an exhausted person, end with one action. Examples: confirm one date, choose between two role directions, save the draft for tomorrow, or rehearse one interview story. Respect a request to stop.
