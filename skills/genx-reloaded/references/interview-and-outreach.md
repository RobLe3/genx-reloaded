# Interviews, outreach and sustainable follow-through

Use only the modules the candidate requests. Reuse the CV's confirmed evidence so dates, ownership, credentials and results remain consistent.

## Interview story

Build a short answer from: situation and period; the person's task; their decision or action; the observed result or deliverable; relevance to this role. If the result is unknown, end with the actual deliverable. Do not invent a happy ending or add a number because interview advice suggests one.

Offer one rehearsal question and concrete feedback on the answer, not a personality score. For a change from management to hands-on work, keep the historical title and explain the candidate's chosen direction. Do not assume willingness to accept lower pay or apologise for experience.

When a question concerns age, health, caring or other private matters, help draft a truthful role-focused answer that respects the person's disclosure preference. Check the applicable country before stating whether a question is unlawful. An accommodation draft can describe the requested adjustment without a diagnosis if that meets the person's needs. Do not send it without authorization.

## Outreach draft

Use a supplied relationship and a specific, modest request. Do not invent familiarity, a referral, or endorsement. A fictional pattern:

"Hello [name], I'm exploring [role direction]. My relevant experience includes [confirmed example]. If you have time, could you tell me which capabilities your team values most for this work?"

Replace placeholders only with known facts; never send an unfinished template. Offer direct applications or community/professional routes when someone has no useful contacts. Do not imply that referrals are compulsory or guarantee a way around screening.

## Work sample and negotiation

Choose a bounded fictional or permission-cleared example. Define scope and time before building it. Keep client secrets, internal financials and third-party intellectual property out. A sample should demonstrate a relevant capability, not deliver a substantial unpaid project for a prospective employer.

Use the person's desired responsibilities and compensation boundaries. Verify market comparisons before quoting them. Do not reduce their target because of age, a career break, or an assumption that they should be grateful.

## Rejection and follow-through

Distinguish explicit feedback, unanswered questions, and speculation. Offer a review or a pause. If tracking is wanted, use application version, role, channel, date, response window and stage. Keep "no response yet", rejection, withdrawal and offer distinct. Counts are observations, not proof that the skill caused an outcome.

Networking research in [evidence section 18](evidence.md#18-networking-context) motivates offering several routes. It does not justify mass messages or a prescribed networking quota.
